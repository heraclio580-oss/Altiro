Hybrid training: running and lifting in one plan

A guide from Altiro · November 2026

Hybrid training means running and lifting in the same week. Lots of people already do both. The trouble is that they usually follow two plans that ignore each other: a running plan that doesn't know you squatted heavy on Thursday, and a gym program that doesn't know Saturday is your long run. Each plan is fine on its own. Together they stack hard days on top of each other, and something gives: tired legs, a skipped run, or a sore week that drags on.

A hybrid plan treats the week as one thing. Here's how Altiro lays it out, and why.

Space the hard days apart

Not every session is equally hard. In Altiro, the Long Run and the fast runs (Tempo Run, Interval Run, Hill Repeats, Fartlek Run) count as hard days. The plan places your week so that two hard days aren't back to back, a heavy Leg Day isn't right before a hard run, and the same muscle group isn't lifted two days running. If you train on a weekend day, the long run goes there.

A sample week with three runs and three lifts looks like this:

DaySession
MonLeg Day · 45 min
TueEasy Run · 3 mi
WedPush Day · 40 min
ThuTempo Run · 4 mi
FriRest Day
SatLong Run · 7 mi

The legs get two full days between the heavy lift and the tempo run, and the long run comes after a rest day.

Most running should be easy

In a hybrid week, the lifting already adds plenty of hard work, so most of your running stays easy. Altiro's cues for each run:

  • Easy Run: zone 2, relaxed, conversational. You should be able to talk in full sentences the whole way.
  • Tempo Run: an easy warm-up, then the tempo part at a comfortably hard pace (you can say a few words at a time, not hold a conversation), then an easy cool-down.
  • Interval Run: after the warm-up, each rep hard but controlled. The last one should feel like the first.
  • Long Run: your longest run of the week. Start easy and stay steady; walk breaks are fine.
  • Easy Jog: an optional 20 to 30 minute jog right after lifting, on days that aren't followed by a hard run.

New runners start with a Brisk Walk and Walk + Jog days, and the jogging grows from what you managed last time.

Lift in a weekly wave

Lifting starts the week light, builds to one peak day where your focus lift goes up to its heaviest, then steps back down, so you finish the week recovered rather than sore. Every set and weight you log feeds the next session's targets.

Build, then deload

Running and lifting both build a little each week. Every 4th week is a deload week: everything comes down a notch (about 85% of a normal week) so you recover and come back stronger. Skipping deloads is a common way to end up stuck or sore.

Plan for life getting in the way

A plan only works if it survives a busy week. Move a missed session to another day, log a workout whenever it actually happened, or change your focus (more running, more lifting) and intensity at any time. Connect Strava and your runs fill in the week on their own, so you see what you've done next to what's left.
